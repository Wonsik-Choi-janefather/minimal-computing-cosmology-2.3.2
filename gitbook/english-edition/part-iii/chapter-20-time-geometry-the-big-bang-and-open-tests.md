# Chapter 20 Time, Geometry, the Big Bang, and Open Tests

In WRRA, time need not be supplied as an external absolute clock. It can be defined through changes in relations among states and correlations with a clock subsystem. If traces of the source clock leak nonuniversally into visible observables, however, the equivalence principle and path independence can fail.

$$
T_rel = T(clock correlations)
$$

_Equation (3.23)_

Geometry can also be reconstructed partly from distinguishability among states rather than treated only as a preinstalled background. An effective metric may be recovered from fidelity or information distance, but this does not automatically guarantee Lorentzian spacetime or Einstein dynamics. Signature, locality, and constraint closure must be checked separately.

The physical degrees of freedom of gravity must likewise be counted after constraints are removed. The number of components in a three-dimensional spatial metric cannot simply be called the number of graviton degrees of freedom. After first-class constraints and gauge orbits are removed, two propagating degrees of freedom per point remain in the 3+1-dimensional Einstein branch.

$$
N_phys = ½(12 − 2×4 − 0) = 2
$$

_Equation (3.24)_

The Big Bang may be interpreted as the renderer's initial boundary or a rank-opening transition. This remains an open hypothesis. Identifying the autonomous opening of the Bessel common carrier with the cosmological Big Bang requires simultaneous agreement with the primordial spectrum, scale invariance, non-Gaussianity, reheating, and standard cosmological data.

The next tests cannot end with tuning numbers into agreement. Calibration and post-test data must be separated, and values not entered as inputs must be predicted among poles, residues, thresholds, gauge observables, flavor data, gravitational waves, and cosmological data.

$$
Prediction = Output(test data not used in calibration)
$$

_Equation (3.25)_

WRRA's open possibilities are not empty space used to evade failure. Anomaly-free quantum constraint closure, the physical state space, observable equivalence among renderers, bounds on source-clock leakage, and independent low-energy predictions are concrete next bosses. The principle that our universe is one possible realization is retained, but candidate explanations of that realization must compete before observation.
