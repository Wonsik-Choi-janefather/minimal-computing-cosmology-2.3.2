# Chapter 15 The Local Standard-Model Parent

In the quantum-realization lineage, a five-dimensional Einstein–scalar parent was presented as WRRA's first explicit local implementation chassis. It stabilizes a finite warped interval with scalar boundary conditions, then places a Bessel radial core and chiral Standard-Model zero modes upon it.

$$
ds² = e^(−2A(y))η_μνdx^μdx^ν + dy²
$$

_Equation (3.9)_

The advantage of this model is that it treats length and gap, chirality and transport together in one local field-theory framework. A finite Kaluza–Klein gap separates the low-energy visible sector from heavy modes, while generation-universal transport is constructed to preserve observed Yukawa, CKM, and PMNS data.

“Preserving,” however, differs from “predicting.” Entering observed Yukawa and mixing data as matching inputs and maintaining them at low energy tests implementability. It does not derive those numbers from geometry alone ab initio.

$$
O_low(μ) = Match_μ\[O_parent; θ_obs\]
$$

_Equation (3.10)_

Nor should the low-energy theory be defined by a naive Kaluza–Klein truncation. Gauge-covariant matching and threshold corrections are needed, and the scalar-deformed sector must be separated from the exact Bessel sector. Stability, anomaly inflow, flavor counterterms, BRST conditions, and the cutoff corridor must also be satisfied.

The exact status of the five-dimensional parent is therefore a possible local implementation chassis. It is neither WRRA's final body nor a structure nature must use, and it is not a fundamental theory valid at every scale.

This limitation is not merely a weakness. Constructing one possible renderer in the language of existing field theory means the architecture does not remain a pure metaphor. The deeper question is now what conditions make distinct implementation paths yield the same observable reality.
