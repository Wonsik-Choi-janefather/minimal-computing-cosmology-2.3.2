# Chapter 53 Nuclear Instability and Five Decay Clocks

Conclusion of this chapter: Known nuclei with Z ≥ 104 are metastable many-body systems that remain bound against nucleon emission while several exits—alpha decay, spontaneous fission, and weak decay—stay open. Total half-life is determined by the fastest remaining channel.

The five clocks are: ① nucleon binding, determined by S\_n, S\_p, S\_2n, and S\_2p; ② weak decay, determined by Q\_β, Q\_EC, and pnQRPA transition strengths; ③ alpha decay, determined by Q\_α, angular momentum, deformation, and F\_α; ④ spontaneous fission, determined by the multidimensional potential-energy surface, collective inertia, and pairing; and ⑤ channel competition, which sums all partial widths. Q > 0 is a necessary condition for a channel to open, not a sufficient condition for its rate.

Sn=M(A−1,Z)+mn−M(A,Z), Sp=M(A−1,Z−1)+mH−M(A,Z) (8.17)

Among 159 known Z ≥ 104 nuclei for which both S\_n and S\_p can be calculated from AME2020/NUBASE2020, none had a nonpositive value. This means that barrier-mediated metastability rather than nucleon unbinding is the issue in the observed sample, but it does not predict undiscovered drip lines.

Among 118 non-estimated half-lives, 109 candidates had a positive weak-decay Q value using central mass values, but only six directions had non-estimated masses for both parent and daughter. Even an open weak-decay branch can be slow because of small weak coupling and spin/parity selection rules, so mass difference alone does not determine half-life.

For 82 alpha-dominant cases, the correlation between Q\_α and log₁₀T\_α was −0.846 \[bootstrap 95% −0.900, −0.772]; for the thirteen exact-Q\_α cases, it was −0.884. For 43 spontaneous-fission cases, the correlation between macroscopic fissility and log₁₀T\_SF was only −0.489 \[−0.683, −0.242]. The former shows Q\_α's strong dominance; the latter shows that Z²/A alone cannot explain the multidimensional fission action.

The difference in median SF partial half-life between even–even and odd–odd samples was 3.70 decades, but is not read as a universal constant in a mixed selected sample. The lifetime ridge near N ≈ 162 is likewise a composite signal of deformed shells, odd–even hindrance, and channel switching; it does not mean that an “island of stability” stops every decay.

Assessment: particle binding, Q\_α dominance, and the fissility limitation in the observed sample are DATA-CONDITIONAL STANDARD NUCLEAR PHYSICS. The 109/118 open weak-decay channels are candidate screening results mostly dependent on estimated masses, and independent Z,N → total-half-life prediction remains OPEN until HFB + microscopic alpha + pnQRPA + competing-width execution is performed jointly.
