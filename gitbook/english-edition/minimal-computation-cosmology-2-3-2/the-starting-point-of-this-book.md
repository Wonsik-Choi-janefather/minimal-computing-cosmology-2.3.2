# The Starting Point of This Book

## Our Universe Is Not Special

Our universe need not be a uniquely selected, finished product among all possible structures. It is not a universe engineered for peak performance according to a special purpose, but one universe that actually survived without collapsing amid change. Accordingly, this book does not invent exceptional rules solely for our universe; it asks whether the same execution grammar applies to other possible structures as well.

## Minimal Computation Is Not an Inevitability but the Most Accessible Survivable Structure

Minimal computation is neither a command to compute unconditionally as little as possible nor a teleology according to which nature must choose a single minimum. It is the principle that, among possible states, a nearby structure that is sufficient to avoid fatal failure and can be reached before a more complex structure is the one that survives. Even a degree of freedom that appears redundant under ordinary conditions belongs to the minimally sufficient structure if it prevents failure below threshold under extreme change.

## There Is Only One Universe, and Only the Present Exists

The past and future do not exist as separate places outside the present universe; one Actual exists as the present state. The past is the Residue and Record remaining in the present, while the future is possibility not yet executed. When the universe loses stability, a separate universe is not born alongside it. The same Actual updates into its next state. If the new state is unstable, it immediately passes into another; if stable, it persists as one state of the universe for as long as that stability endures.

## Finite, Yet Without Beginning or End

Each stable state of the universe may be finite, but the totality of state transitions through which one Actual continues requires neither a first nor a last moment imposed from outside. The Möbius strip is not asserted to be the topology of the actual universe. It is an analogy showing how the absolute boundary between beginning and end can disappear within a finite structure.

## Time Is Experienced the Moment Consciousness Arises

Even without consciousness, the order and rate of physical states exist, and clocks measure intervals between events. Yet there is no subject that feels waiting, age, or the flow from before to after. In this sense, to a being without consciousness, all change in the universe is like a single instant given in one present. When consciousness arises, it connects the record of the present to memory and anticipation, thereby experiencing the same physical order as duration and flow.
