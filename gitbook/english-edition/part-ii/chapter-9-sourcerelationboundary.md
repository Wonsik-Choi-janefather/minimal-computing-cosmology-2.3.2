# Chapter 9 SOURCE–RELATION–BOUNDARY

At the dimensional-filter stage, the SOURCE–RELATION–BOUNDARY trichotomy was used. In the final model, however, possible components and the state space, RELATION's couplings, symmetries, and conservation laws, and BOUNDARY's constraints and recording conditions all belong to LAW. SOURCE is not identical to this LAW; it is a boundary input that acts on the fixed structure to open one history.

SOURCE must not be understood as a warehouse of matter or a repository of all possibilities. Possibility and degrees of freedom reside in the state space defined by LAW; SOURCE is the minimal boundary input acting on a particular opening degree of freedom in that space. BOUNDARY rules are likewise part of LAW and specify what canonical jump SOURCE η produces.

RELATION is not a simple line joining two layers. It includes symmetry, topology, coupling, selection rules, conservation laws, and transport kernels. It distinguishes which source states are the same or different and determines which combinations can be realized together and which are forbidden.

_ARCH = (LAW\[STATE, RELATION, BOUNDARY, RENDERER], SOURCE\[η]) (2.8)_

Different universes are described by the combination of a realizable space determined by LAW's symmetries, couplings, constants, boundaries, and renderer, and a SOURCE input that begins one history in each such space. Our universe is one realization among the allowed combinations that agrees with observation.

_U\_i = Execute(LAW\_i, SOURCE\_i; open events\_i) (2.9)_

## SOURCE Does Not Directly Store Every Answer

For each question X, the information ledger records the original owner, minimum input, execution rule, realization trigger, recording format, status grade, and precision. Rather than storing only an answer as one number, it preserves where that number came from.

P(X)=(owner, payload, execution map, trigger, record, status, precision)

Here, the owner is the layer that first possesses independent information. Laws, constants, global topology, symmetries, couplings, representation rules, and boundary instruments belong to the LAW HEADER; the opening impulse η belongs to SOURCE; evolution of the present state belongs to the execution engine; and a particular observational record belongs to the renderer and event instrument. Storing the same information redundantly at several layers violates the principle of minimal computation.

The “one-dimensional point as a minimal unit” in the final model is not a small physical point placed in three-dimensional space, but an abstract state or relation address defined by LAW. The three directions of visible space are a structural output produced when WRRA's dimensional filter transforms relations into representation space. If global topology, the initial curvature scale, or boundary data are not derived dynamically, they must remain in the ledger as LAW or explicit boundary data and must not be confused with SOURCE η.

Initially, this trichotomy seemed sufficient. A problem appeared as soon as physical objects emerged: the intermediate process through which source possibility becomes a boundary record had been compressed too heavily into one word. The mere existence of relations did not jointly realize mass, charge, spin, and position as one object.

This omission later acquired the name RENDERER. Before reaching it, however, the filter's static structure had to be made dynamical, and multiple internal channels had to be bound under one transport action.
