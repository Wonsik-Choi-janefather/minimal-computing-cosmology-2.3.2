# Chapter 12 Hardware–Firmware–Software–Execution

One reason complex physical models become unstable is that they mix the responsibilities of different explanatory layers. The WRRA lineage divides these into four layers: Hardware, Firmware, Software, and Execution. This is an analogy separating structural ownership, not a claim that a computer exists outside the universe.

Hardware determines what exists and which directions can be connected, including representations and charge ratios, the fifteen channels, and common transport. Changing this layer changes the particle content and the allowed representations themselves.

Firmware governs spectral weights, the metric of kinetic terms, pole activation, matching, and vacuum selection. Even with the same Hardware, this layer determines which modes are visible at low energy and which couplings are effective.

Software governs concrete patterns of matter, including Yukawa structure, generations, mixing angles, and CP phases. Merely relabeling observed masses and mixings and moving them into this layer is only re-expression. To count as a new theoretical result, it must reduce independent inputs or calculate a relation not previously used.

$$
M_Y = Y(Hardware, Firmware; θ_software)
$$

_Equation (2.15)_

Execution is the calculation that brings this structure to the actual observational scale, through renormalization-group flow, thresholds, and pole masses. Even when observables are said to be matched after quantum realization, numerical fitting must be distinguished from physical execution. This is why calibration data and post hoc test data are separated.

$$
O_obs(μ) = RG_μ ∘ Threshold ∘ Pole\[M_Y\]
$$

_Equation (2.16)_

This hierarchy locates research failures instead of hiding them. Hardware can be correct while Software remains open, and a tree-level relation can fail at Execution. Conversely, reproducing observations well does not prove the Hardware principle.
