# Chapter 31 The Visible Port

The Euclidean response of the continuous spectral target is read as a Stieltjes transform. For Q² ≥ 0, G\_E(Q²) is the integral of the spectral density divided by Q²+s. In the finite Jacobi model, the same quantity is the diagonal matrix element of the resolvent with respect to e₀.

$$
G_E(Q²)=∫ ρ(s)/(Q²+s) ds
$$

_Equation (5.5)_

After diagonalization, the finite response is the sum of w\_j/(Q²+s\_j). This representation shows that the quadrature nodes and weights are not merely a numerical list, but arise from one self-adjoint Hamiltonian and visible vector.

The same data also own the real-time amplitude. The continuous target is A(t) = e^(−it)(1+it)^(−3/2), and the finite model is A\_N(t) = Σ\_j w\_j e^(−is\_jt). The Euclidean response and real-time amplitude are not fitted with different parameters.

$$
A(t)=e^(−it)(1+it)^(−3/2), A_N(t)=Σ_j w_j e^(−is_jt)
$$

_Equation (5.6)_

The fact that one port reads every node means that information is compressed to rank one; it does not mean that the entire matter space is one-dimensional. The hidden Jacobi chain has state dimension N, while visible coupling occurs only through e₀.

This common-owner structure meets the minimal-computation intuition of WRRA. Instead of attaching a separate sensor and carrier to each component of the continuum, one collective boundary coordinate carries the common spectral content.

This visible port, however, is not yet a Standard-Model particle with charge, spin, and flavor. It is a minimal interface reading the spectrum without loss; the joint realization of physical quantum numbers remains a problem for a larger embedding.
