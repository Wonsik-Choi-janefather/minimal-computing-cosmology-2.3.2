The final axiomatic declaration places classical mechanics, relativity, quantum mechanics, and WRRA side by side not as a ranking of successes and failures, but as different questions posed to reality. Classical mechanics asks about motion; relativity, spacetime and causal structure; quantum mechanics, amplitudes and probabilities of possibilities; and WRRA, the structure through which an allowed possibility is realized as one stable reality.

In one line: “motion → spacetime → possibility → realization.” The notation Quantum Mechanics ⊂ WRRA is not a theorem asserting that all quantum mathematics is set-theoretically a subset of WRRA. ① Actual–Reality distinction: visible reality is a restricted phenotype of the larger Actual evolving according to LAW. ② LAW's support set: probability does not create possibility but weights only candidates permitted by causality, conservation, charge, representation, interaction history, and records. ③ Quantum refinement: superposition, interference, entanglement, tunneling, and Born weights operate normally within allowed histories.

④ Open events: laws may be fixed while the outcomes of individual events are not written in advance. ⑤ Record-based realization: observation is not human consciousness, but formation of a stable record physically distinguishing alternatives. ⑥ Macroscopic continuity: a macroscopic history stabilized by many records is not replaced by another history without a lawful process.

⑦ Action–record boundary: the boundary between microscopic possibility and macroscopic fact depends not on mass alone but on accumulated action and environmental records; the present candidate is Ω\_WRRA = R\_rec McΔx/h. ⑧ Information conservation and phenotypic deficit: information absent from Reality need not have vanished; it may remain in hidden or environmental correlations.

## The Gap of a Single Outcome

A subsequent boundary calculation made clear that a single outcome fails to emerge not because decoherence is insufficient, but because decoherence and outcome selection are different operations. Coupling a premeasurement state to an environment through a time-independent self-adjoint Hamiltonian permits unitary record formation of the following form.

U(|ψ⟩⊗|E₀⟩) = Σ\_a c\_a |a⟩⊗|E\_a⟩, ⟨E\_a|E\_b⟩≈δ\_ab

This construction can simultaneously conserve energy, preserve Born branch weights |c\_a|², and orthogonalize environmental records. Tracing out the environment makes interference terms in the reduced state disappear, so it resembles a classical mixture, but the total state Σ\_a c\_a|a,E\_a⟩ still contains every branch.

No deterministic selector can, using only linear unitary dynamics, map every input superposition to one of its branches while simultaneously preserving linearity, norm, and Born weights. Appending one branch as an actual event requires at least one additional element: conditional update by an instrument, stochastic selection, nonlinear dynamics, or a separate ontological rule.

This result closes the shortcut “decoherence is collapse,” but does not refute every possible single-outcome theory. The exact NO-GO applies only to linear unitary dynamics by itself; the microscopic selector remains OPEN. The route that inserts consciousness or will as an ad hoc collapse port is also left inactive in the present interface because it lacks a physical coupling and testable law.

## Possibilities Are Plural, but Actuality Is Singular

An open future need not be identified with many-worlds branching. In WRRA's single-history hypothesis, several next events may be allowed at one time, but exactly one result enters the actual physical record. Unselected results remain counterfactual possibilities defining subsequent probabilities; they are not assumed to continue as independent physical universes.

Ψₙ → {a₁,…,aₖ}, Aₙ∈{a₁,…,aₖ}, hₙ=(hₙ₋₁,Aₙ) (6.7)

This model therefore differs both from classical determinism, in which initial values already contain the entire outcome, and from the Everettian many-worlds interpretation, in which every outcome is realized in a different world. Before realization, the future is open as conditional possibility; after an event is recorded, it closes as one irreversible past.
