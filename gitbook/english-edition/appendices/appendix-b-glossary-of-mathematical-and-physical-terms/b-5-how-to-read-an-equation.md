# B-5 How to Read an Equation

1. First inspect the left side of the equality. Identify what the equation ultimately seeks to calculate.
2. Divide the symbols on the right into inputs, rules, and outputs. Mark values imported from prior observations as “inputs” and newly calculated values as “outputs.”
3. Check the units. If quantities with different units, such as length and energy, are simply added, the equation is likely wrong.
4. Determine whether it is exact or approximate. Symbols such as ≈, a truncated mode count, and small-parameter assumptions limit the domain of application.
5. Separate mathematical validity from physical interpretation. Calculating that a determinant is zero and claiming that an actual particle was created require different evidence.
6. Finally, identify the falsification condition. A hypothesis is stronger when it clearly states what observational or computational result would require its rejection.
